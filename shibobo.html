What's in the game

Real streets, real pitch

Every match opens over Bophirima Avenue in Dobsonville. The pitch, the streets and the houses around it come from OpenStreetMap, then the camera drops into the cage for kick-off.

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors

Shibobo. Tsamaya. Rainbow.

Nine skills on a flick of your right thumb, and every one has its name painted over your player when it lands:

  • Shibobo
  • Tsamaya
  • Rainbow
  • Roulette
  • Drag-back
  • Step-over
  • Heel flick
  • Flick-up
  • Knock-on

The shibobo is the nutmeg: through his legs and gone.

Fill the Gees meter

Style fills the Gees meter, but only with an audience: a trick with nobody near you counts for a third, and the same trick twice counts for less. Fill it and you get eighteen seconds of Gees mode: harder shots and a yard of pace. Score in it and the other crew's meter is wiped.

A PS5-style pad on the glass

A stick on the left, face buttons on the right, shoulder buttons up top, laid out the way your thumbs already know. Or pair a real controller over Bluetooth (a DualSense, an Xbox pad or most Android pads) and the glass pad steps aside.

Four-a-side with keepers against the CPU: three minutes, or first to five.

Skool: fourteen drills

One drill for every control and every skill, in teaching order. Each card says what to do and how, counts your reps, and moves you on when it's done.

Play without Wi-Fi

Shibobo installs from the browser in under a minute and is about 1 MB. After the first open it needs no data at all.

iPhone

  1. Open okuvezi-dotcom.github.io/shibobo in Safari.
  2. Tap Share ⇪, then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  3. Open Shibobo from the Home Screen once while you're online. That's when it saves itself to the phone.
  4. After that it plays in airplane mode. Turn the phone sideways.

Android

  1. Open the game in Chrome.
  2. Tap Install when Chrome offers it, or open the ⋮ menu and choose Add to Home screen.
  3. Open it from the home screen once while you're online.
  4. After that it plays with no signal.

Team talk

Does it really play offline?

Yes. Once you've opened it from your home screen while online, the whole game is on your phone: the players, the cage and the Dobsonville streets. Airplane mode is fine.

What does it cost?

Nothing. There are no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to make.

How much data does it use?

About 1 MB, once. When you're online it quietly checks for a newer version and fetches it in the background; it never interrupts a match to do that.

What does it know about me?

Nothing. It remembers which Skool drills you've finished, on your phone, and that's all. Nothing is sent anywhere. The privacy policy has the detail.

Can I use a real controller?

Yes. Pair it with your phone over Bluetooth first. A DualSense, an Xbox controller and most Android pads use the standard layout the game reads, with the same buttons and the same right-stick skill flicks as the on-screen pad.

Is the pitch real? Are the teams?

The pitch and its streets are real: Bophirima Avenue in Dobsonville, Soweto, from OpenStreetMap. The crews (Dube Stars, Mofolo Mamba) and the businesses on the hoardings are made up.

Which phones does it run on?

Recent iPhones and Android phones, held sideways. It also runs in a desktop browser, where the keyboard stands in for the pad.
