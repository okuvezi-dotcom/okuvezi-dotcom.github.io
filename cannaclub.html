Built around four constraints

A food-delivery app with a cannabis menu would fail on day one. Four facts shape almost every design decision, and each has a written architecture decision behind it.

  1. The catalogue can't be public.

    Membership and age verification gate what a person can see, on the server. A member with no approved membership sees no products anywhere: not in search, not in a link, not in a live update.

  2. Card networks won't process cannabis.

    Payments go over bank rails only (PayShap and instant EFT, through two providers behind one port). An order reaches the club only after the provider's signed confirmation that the money has landed.

  3. App stores ban cannabis ordering.

    Members get an installable web app, so no store review can switch it off. Only the driver app is native, and it is a logistics app: no catalogue, no payments.

  4. The audit trail is the evidence.

    Every compliance decision lands in an append-only, hash-chained ledger. Each entry's SHA-256 covers its own contents and the previous entry's hash, so a quiet edit breaks every hash after it.

The member's path, checked at every step

Screens from the design mockup. The sign-in, identity and membership steps are built and enforced by the server today; the doorstep screens show the designed flow.

  1. Prove the age

    The SA ID number is checked as the server checks it: thirteen digits, the checksum, the date of birth, eighteen or over. Consent for the identity check is captured first, for that purpose only.

  2. See the club, not the shelf

    A verified adult can see that clubs exist and apply to one. What a club holds stays invisible until that club's own committee approves the membership.

  3. The club decides

    The member signs the club's own agreement, in the exact version shown. Approval comes from the club, not the platform, and the membership number is what the driver checks at the door.

  4. No money, no order

    Until the bank confirms the payment, the club can't see the order at all. Not pending: invisible. Dispatching on an unconfirmed payment is ruled out by the order's state machine, not by a convention.

  5. ID at the door

    The driver checks the member's ID against the membership before handing anything over. If it's someone else, the handoff fails and is recorded, and the order goes back to the club.

Who is party to the money

Each club is the principal in its own member transactions. Members pay the club's own bank account; the platform never holds members' payments for goods. The platform invoices clubs separately, for software and delivery logistics, rather than taking a percentage of what they sell.

Both money flows sit behind one settlement seam, and fee plans are data rather than code, so if counsel advises a different structure it is a configuration change, not a rewrite. That reduces the platform's exposure. It does not remove it, and we don't pretend otherwise.

Legal review is part of go-live

South Africa's Cannabis for Private Purposes Act (2024) covers private adult use, cultivation and sharing. It did not create a licensed retail market, which is why clubs operate as closed private associations. The structure described on this page has not been tested in South African law. Before any club takes live money:

  • an attorney with cannabis and payments experience reviews the transaction structure, the club agreement, the membership agreement and the fee basis;
  • the payment providers confirm in writing that they will onboard the clubs for this activity;
  • a decision is made, on advice, on whether drivers are employed by the platform or contracted through the clubs.

Not on the app stores, by design

Apple's App Review Guidelines and Google Play's policies both prohibit apps that facilitate buying cannabis, including ordering it for delivery. So the member app is an installable web app that members add to their home screen, and it is not submitted to either store. The driver app (assignments, navigation, proof of delivery) carries no catalogue and takes no payment.

Questions operators ask

Can I buy cannabis here?

No. This site is for club operators and investors. CannaClub is software that clubs use with their own verified members, and there is nothing for sale on this site or in the mockup.

Is it legal?

Private adult use, cultivation and sharing are covered by the Cannabis for Private Purposes Act, 2024; commercial retail is not. The platform is designed for closed members' clubs and structured as a software and logistics provider, but that structure needs review by a South African attorney before any live money, and that review is part of going live. This page is not legal advice.

What happens to members' ID documents?

They go to a verification provider with the member's consent, for that purpose only. As designed, the platform keeps the ID number and date of birth; document images are held encrypted and purged on a schedule. The compliance ledger records references and outcomes only, never ID numbers or documents, because it is permanent and can't take part in POPIA erasure.

Is the ledger tamper-proof?

It's tamper-evident, which is the honest claim. Updates and deletes are refused at three layers (the domain model, the ledger's interface and a database trigger), and an edit that gets past all three breaks every later hash. Someone with owner rights over the database could still rewrite history and recompute the chain; publishing the head hash somewhere the operator doesn't control closes that, and it's on the list before launch.

Why not card payments, or cash on delivery?

Card acquirers generally won't process cannabis and tend to close accounts with funds held. Cash puts money on drivers, which is a safety and reconciliation problem. Bank rails settle bank to bank, and the platform waits for the provider's signed confirmation before an order moves.
